# Film continuity

A film feels professional when the audience never notices a seam. The art director locks a continuity bible before
anyone animates; the director plans inside it; every animator copies it exactly; the critic rejects drift.

## The bible (locked once, used everywhere)

- Medium: 3D (world3d), 2D toon, stick figure, or none. A film never changes medium between scenes. Title cards and
  end cards may be pure type, but any character on screen is drawn in the film's medium.
- Model sheet: each character's exact parameters (preset, hair, colours, outfit, extras, props). Copy them character for
  character into every scene; add only position, facing, actions and expression.
- World: the recurring place, its palette, time of day and light direction. If a scene moves somewhere else, it is a
  deliberate story beat and the director says so.
- Look: one colour grade for the film; one camera language (lens lengths, moves) unless the story changes.

## Between cuts

- Screen direction: a character exiting right enters the next shot from the left. Chases keep one direction.
- 180-degree rule: in a conversation, keep the camera on one side of the line between the characters.
- Eyelines: if A looks right at B in one shot, B looks left at A in the reverse.
- Match on action: cut in the middle of a movement and continue it in the next shot.
- State carries over: a dropped prop stays dropped, singed hair stays singed, a door left open stays open, until the story
  changes it. The director writes the state into each scene's visual.
- Scale and lighting match: the same character is not suddenly twice as tall, and the key light stays on the same side
  in one location.

## Planning shots like a film

- Establish (wide) before you cut in (medium, close). Return to a wide after a run of close shots.
- One idea per shot; each shot ends on a frame the next one can answer.
- Vary shot size between consecutive shots (wide, medium, close) to keep cuts invisible.
- Reaction shots sell the joke and the emotion: always give the listener a reaction.

## Self-check before finishing a scene

1. Is every character drawn with the film's component and exact model-sheet parameters?
2. Does the set, time of day and light match the previous scene?
3. Does screen direction and state continue from the previous scene's last frame?
